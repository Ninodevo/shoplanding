Why one product
deserves
one perfect page.

A field guide to the seven blocks, sixty-nine rules, and one stubborn idea behind ShopLanding.

By The ShopLanding Editors · 12 min read · Illustrations by The Renderer

The thesis.

Every store owner who has ever launched a single product knows the same anxious feeling: the page is up, the ads are running, and somehow it is converting at 1.2%. The fix is rarely the headline. It is rarely the photography. It is, almost always, that the page is missing two or three of the boring blocks a buyer needs to feel safe.1

ShopLanding is the bet that those boring blocks are knowable, orderable, and shippable. The product is a system: seven blocks of canonical anatomy distilled from sixty-nine public conversion rules, packaged as Shopify and WooCommerce theme zips and a portable system specification any agency can re-implement.

The marketing site you are reading is the proof. Every section of every page on this site is one of the blocks we sell. The stack is the argument.

Buyers don’t shop on price for this. They want a defensibly good baseline so they can spend their attention on the product. — The product brief, January 2026

Why “opinionated” is the feature.

Most theme marketplaces sell flexibility. We sell its opposite. The order of the seven blocks is fixed because conversion is fragile to disorder — you cannot put social proof above the buy box and expect it to do the same work. The system enforces sequence so the buyer’s decision arc is always: what is it, what does it look like, what does it cost, who else bought it, why is it worth more, and what objections do I have left.

The CTA area alone carries twenty-three of the rules in the system — variants, quantity, BNPL eligibility, urgency, scarcity, live activity, express pay, free-ship progress, subscription, and the trust micro-icons that quietly carry conversion across the line.2

The economics.

ShopLanding is sold once. There is no subscription tier, no free plan, no urgency timer. The single-store license is $99. The unlimited-stores license is $249. The done-for-you setup is a one-time +$199 add-on. Every license carries lifetime updates for the stores it covers.

Why one-time? Because the product is “a system you ship,” not “a tool you operate.” A subscription would require us to keep adding rules forever; instead, we keep adding niche presets and let buyers carry them in. Skincare, supplements, food, candles, gadgets — same anatomy, different tokens, different default copy.

Why three tiers? Because operators and agencies have different math than founders. A founder with one product wants a $99 ceiling. An agency that ships ten DTC builds a year would happily pay $249 to skip relicensing. Setup as an add-on captures everyone in the middle who would rather pay than read the docs.3

The quiet case for ownership.

The unlimited license includes a portable system specification — a JSON structure of the block taxonomy plus Markdown notes on every rule. Your team can re-implement the same anatomy on Hydrogen, Astro, Webflow, anywhere. The page system survives any platform decision, and any future re-platform.

That is the quiet case for buying ShopLanding once: the spec is yours, the audit is documented, and the next agency you hire inherits the same defaults. The conversion baseline does not regress because the new team did not know the order.

It is, in other words, an institutional memory you can buy for $249.
