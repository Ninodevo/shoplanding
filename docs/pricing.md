# Pricing

## Model

**One-time license, three line items per theme.** No subscription.

| SKU | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Single-store license | **$99** | Use on one Shopify or WooCommerce store. Lifetime updates for that store. |
| Unlimited-stores license | **$249** | Use on every store you'll ever build. Lifetime updates. |
| Done-for-you setup (add-on) | **+$199** | I install the theme on your store, wire payments, and write 5 sections of copy from a brief. |

Stored on `Theme` as `priceSingleCents` (`9900`), `priceUnlimitedCents` (`24900`), `setupAddOnCents` (`19900`).

## Why this model

We considered three:

- **A. Flat one-time** ✅ chosen
- **B. One-time + paid annual updates** — adds renewal-reminder work for thin gain.
- **C. Subscription** ($19/mo or $149/yr) — wrong mental model for "I'm launching one product, I want to pay once."

**Reasons for A:**
1. Matches buyer intent — these are one-product launches, not ongoing tooling subscriptions.
2. Two tiers is enough price discrimination. Solo founders pick $99; agencies/freelancers serving multiple clients pick $249.
3. The setup add-on is the real margin lever — it converts our deepest knowledge (the playbook) into a high-margin service offer that uses the tweaks panel as the intake form.
4. We can always add a subscription tier later (once there are ≥5 themes and returning buyers). Going the other direction is harder.

## What's *not* part of pricing

- No free tier. The playbook is freely readable; the artifacts are paid.
- No discounts, urgency timers, or "Black Friday" theatre on the marketing site. Confidence sells better than fake scarcity.
- No "Enterprise / Contact us" gate. Two tiers, displayed.
- No usage-based pricing. The same zip works on one store or one hundred — that's why the $249 unlimited tier exists.

## Refunds

14-day refund, no questions, on the digital license. The setup add-on is refundable until work begins.

(Documented here for product clarity; the legal copy lives on the marketing site's FAQ and footer.)

## When to revisit

- After 50 paid orders: look at single-vs-unlimited mix. If <10% pick unlimited, the price gap is wrong.
- After we have 5 themes: re-evaluate whether to add a $19/mo "all themes + updates" tier.
- After we know per-customer support load: decide if the unlimited tier should include a dedicated Slack channel or stay self-serve.
